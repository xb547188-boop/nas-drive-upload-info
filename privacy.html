Privacy policy

For NAS Google Drive Upload. Last updated: 10 October 2026.

Purpose and data use

This is a private application operated by its owner to upload a configured NAS folder to the owner's selected Google Drive folder. It uses file names, paths, sizes, modification times, file contents, and checksums to compare files, upload new or changed content, and verify successful uploads.

Google authorization

Google OAuth provides access and refresh tokens so the scheduled task can access Drive without repeated sign-in. rclone requests the Google Drive scope, which permits viewing, creating, updating, and deleting Drive files. The current upload task confines operations to its configured destination and uses copy rather than deletion-based mirroring.

Storage and transmission

Uploaded files are stored in Google Drive under the owner's account and follow that destination's existing sharing settings. Credentials and operational logs are stored locally on the owner's NAS. Network connections to Google use HTTPS through a dedicated proxy. The proxy is an intermediary for encrypted traffic, not a public file destination.

Sharing and other uses

The configured application does not sell data, send files to advertising or analytics services, or use uploaded files to train AI models. This public information website contains no NAS files, OAuth tokens, proxy credentials, or operational logs.

Control and revocation

The owner can stop the NAS task and revoke its access in Google Account third-party connections. Revoking authorization stops future access; uploaded files remain in Drive until the owner removes them there. The owner controls local configuration and logs. Website-hosting and Google services have their own privacy practices.

Contact

For this private application, contact its operator using the support email displayed on the Google OAuth consent screen.
